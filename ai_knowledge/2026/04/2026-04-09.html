Graph Query Generation with Constraint-guided Large Language Agents
约束引导大语言智能体的图查询生成
KGQA graph query generation Cypher constraint reasoning LLM agents
UniQGen uses LLM agents to extract and refine graph query clauses into executable Cypher, extending Chase & Backchase with dynamic constraint reasoning and query-quality estimation. On a Cypher-supported Freebase graph deployed on Amazon Neptune it outperforms prior graph-query generation methods with F1 gains of 31.6% on GraphQ and 4.9% on GrailQA without fine-tuning for schema matching.

arxiv:2604.08510 arXiv ✓
What do Language Models Learn and When? The Implicit Curriculum Hypothesis
语言模型学到什么、何时学到？隐式课程假说
pretraining curriculum skill emergence function vectors model internals training trajectories
Tracking simple composable tasks across four model families from 410M to 13B parameters, the authors find that emergence orderings are strikingly consistent (ρ = .81 across 45 model pairs) and that composite tasks most often emerge after their component tasks. The representation space derived from these tasks predicts training trajectories of held-out compositional tasks with R² = .68–.84 without prior evaluation, supporting the Implicit Curriculum Hypothesis that pretraining follows a compositional curriculum.

STaR-DRO: Stateful Tsallis Reweighting for Group-Robust Structured Prediction
STaR-DRO：面向群体鲁棒结构化预测的状态化 Tsallis 重加权
structured prediction ontology constraints group robustness chain-of-thought prompting clinical NLP
The paper combines a modular prompt architecture—XML-style structure, expert rules, chain-of-thought, schema contracts and a self-validation gate—with STaR-DRO, a group-robust reweighting method that upweights only persistently hard groups. On the EPPC Miner clinical structured-prediction task, prompt engineering adds 14.46 label F1 and 17.40 span F1 on average across 1B–70B Llama models, and STaR-DRO further improves accuracy while reducing mean groupwise validation cross-entropy by 21.3% and 14.8% relative to SFT and standard DRO.

arxiv:2605.27380 arXiv ✓
BioELX: Context-Aware Cross-lingual Biomedical Entity Linking without Task-Specific Supervision
BioELX：无需任务特定监督的上下文感知跨语言生物医学实体链接
entity linking cross-lingual retrieval mention-anchored prompting biomedical knowledge base reranking
BioELX continues training SapBERT_multi with Wikidata-derived cross-lingual alias supervision to build shared concept neighborhoods, then adapts LLM rerankers with mention-anchored prompting so candidates are scored against the intended mention. It reports state-of-the-art results on four cross-lingual biomedical entity-linking benchmarks, improving Recall@1 by 4.9 to 18.2 points without task-specific BEL annotations.
→ arxiv.org
arxiv:2604.09741 arXiv ✓
ExecTune: Effective Steering of Black-Box LLMs with Guide Models
ExecTune：用引导模型有效操控黑盒 LLM
guide-core policies black-box LLM inference cost structured strategies execution accuracy
ExecTune trains a guide model to produce structured strategies that a black-box core model executes, optimizing executability through teacher-guided acceptance sampling, supervised fine-tuning and structure-aware reinforcement learning. Across math and code benchmarks it improves accuracy by up to 9.2% over prior state of the art while cutting inference cost by up to 22.4%, enabling Claude Haiku 3.5 to outperform Sonnet 3.5 and come within 1.7 points of Sonnet 4 at 38% lower cost.

arxiv:2604.08801 arXiv ✓
$p1$: Better Prompt Optimization with Fewer Prompts
$p1$：用更少提示实现更好的提示优化
prompt optimization prompt selection reward variance reasoning benchmarks
The authors decompose reward variance across system prompts into response stochasticity and system-prompt quality, arguing that prompt optimization succeeds when system-prompt variance dominates. Their p1 method filters for a small subset of user prompts with high variance across candidate system prompts; training on only two AIME 24 prompts yields a system prompt that generalizes well to other reasoning benchmarks and outperforms strong baselines such as GEPA.
