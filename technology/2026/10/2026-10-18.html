Representations in Motion: Layer-wise Rotation and Trajectory Divergence in LLMs
运动中的表征：LLM 的逐层旋转与轨迹分歧
interpretability factuality representation geometry LLM
The paper examines how representations of factuality evolve layer by layer when a model is prompted to lie or to be honest, and reports that the factuality directions extracted from the two conditions rotate away from each other across layers. Similar rotation appears in a task requiring different 3D visual perspectives, and single-trial trajectories are shown to predict the model's target task and behavior.

FTFM: Factorized Tabular Foundation Models
FTFM：因子化表格基础模型
tabular foundation models TabArena in-context learning
FTFM is a tabular foundation model that preserves cell-level representations while factorizing computation through one contextual latent per row and one per column, so cells no longer attend to each other directly. It reports competitive tabular classification results on benchmarks including TabArena and BeyondArena.

Learning Which Device to Call: Online Tool Grounding for LLM Agents in IoT
学会调用哪台设备：IoT 中 LLM Agent 的在线工具接地
tool grounding IoT contextual bandits differential privacy
The paper studies online tool grounding for LLM agents in IoT, where one requested capability is offered by several devices and the agent's plan does not specify which one to call. It formulates device selection as a contextual bandit problem and reports higher step success and better cost-adjusted service, at lower simulated energy and latency, than nearest-device, UCB1 and LinUCB baselines in a simulated loading-dock workload.

DiffGround：语义差异引导的零样本视频问题定位
video question grounding zero-shot multimodal LLM SigLIP2

基于散度的 LLM 排序偏好对齐
preference alignment f-divergence ranked feedback Qwen

ViMoLa: Discrete Motion Tokens for Language-Guided Video Generation
ViMoLa：用于语言引导视频生成的离散运动 token
video generation motion tokens vector quantization
ViMoLa predicts a motion prior from language and injects it into a frozen image-to-video backbone, compressing dense trajectories into 64 tokens from a 512-entry codebook and predicting them with a 28M-parameter transformer. Trained on 462K videos from Something-Something v2 and Kinetics-400, it raises directional accuracy from 0.287 to 0.442 against a 0.250 chance baseline, while the authors note that control remains imperfect.
