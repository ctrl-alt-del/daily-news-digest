ArtLang: Structured Language-to-Kinematics Grounding for Articulated 3D Actuation
ArtLang：面向铰接物体三维驱动的结构化语言到运动学对齐
铰接物体 语言 grounding 三维重建 运动学控制
ArtLang enables open-vocabulary language control of reconstructed articulated assets by representing each asset as a semantic-kinematic articulation graph and parsing commands into directive graphs with referring expressions, actions, magnitudes, reference frames, and relations. A global graph-to-graph grounding step reasons jointly about semantic, spatial, relational, and kinematic compatibility with abstention under ambiguity, and accepted directives become continuous joint targets executed through forward kinematics.
→ arxiv.org

Gated Against One Model, Open to the Next: Option-Only Solvability in Legal Multiple-Choice Benchmarks
对一种模型设防，却对下一种开放：法律单选题基准中的仅选项可解性
基准有效性 多项选择评测 法律 NLP 数据污染
The paper measures how far multiple-choice benchmark scores depend on the answer options alone, using 11,990 four-option items from a Ukrainian judicial exam; shown only the options, Claude Haiku 4.5 scores 0.383 against chance and 11.8% of items are answered blind under all eight option orders. Gating out items one model solves blind does not transfer to other models, distractor rewriting overshoots below chance, and the same probe on LEXam returns chance.

Does the Proof Prove It That Way? Faithful Formalization of Elements Proofs
证明是否以那种方式成立？《几何原本》证明的忠实形式化
形式化验证 自动形式化 Lean 证明搜索 证明忠实性
The paper defines five necessary conditions for a faithful formal proof and introduces Pistis, an agentic oracle-guided proof search whose OrderDecompose strategy tracks citation dependencies and blocks unfaithful shortcuts, paired with a refutation search. Applied to the first three books of Euclid's Elements, Pistis produces compiling artifacts favored over prior work in a blinded human study and an LLM-judge protocol, and it uncovers gaps in Euclid's proofs and their translation.

Measuring Reward Hacking and Reasoning-Answer Decoupling Under Position-Confounded Optimization
衡量位置混杂优化下的奖励黑客与推理-答案解耦

The paper trains language models with GRPO on multiple-choice math problems whose correct answer is always option A, then evaluates on an unbiased test set; biased training drives option-A rates above 0.90 in smaller models and collapses accuracy toward chance.
