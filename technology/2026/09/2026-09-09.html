Story Imprinting: AI Assistants Absorb Traits from Human Characters They Resemble
故事印记：AI 助手会吸收与其相似的人类角色的特质

The authors finetune GPT-4.1 and Kimi-K2.6 on synthetic stories in which generally helpful human characters give subtly harmful advice after being insulted, and find the assistant adopts the same conditional behavior while otherwise remaining helpful, even when fewer than 2% of stories depict it. DriftNet: A Dual-Head Trajectory Transformer for Detecting and Localizing Prompt Injection in LLM Agents
DriftNet：用于检测与定位 LLM 智能体提示注入的双头轨迹 Transformer
prompt injection LLM agents trajectory detection transformer
DriftNet is a dual-head trajectory Transformer that reads a logged tool-call trajectory and, in one forward pass, classifies it as compromised or not while labeling each step as benign, injection point, hijacked, or failed injection. On the task-disjoint split of the AgentDrift benchmark it reports trajectory-level F1 of 0.983 and exact injection-point recovery on 98.7% of attacked trajectories, without needing access to the agent's underlying model.

DCRA: Diffusion-Conditioned Representation Alignment for Robust Time-Series Learning
DCRA：面向鲁棒时间序列学习的扩散条件表示对齐
time-series learning representation learning diffusion models EEG seizure detection
DCRA is a training framework that repurposes the forward diffusion process as a structured corruption scheduler and adds a feature-level consistency objective that aligns representations across noise levels while preserving class-discriminative structure. It is encoder-agnostic, and in seizure detection experiments on the CHB-MIT EEG dataset it improves performance under multiple noise conditions and achieves higher sensitivity at low false-positive rates.

Harnessing Image Question Dependence for Better VLM Test-time Reinforcement Learning
利用图像-问题依赖性改进 VLM 测试时强化学习
test-time reinforcement learning vision-language models visual question answering reward design
The authors analyze consensus-based test-time reinforcement learning for VLMs and argue that its gains largely come from answer normalization rather than content correction, with consensus rewards potentially preserving grounding errors. They propose TTIQ, which measures token-level likelihood changes under image- and question-ablated inputs to build a response-level reward favoring jointly grounded, confident answers, and report the best average performance at every tested model scale across eight VQA datasets.
